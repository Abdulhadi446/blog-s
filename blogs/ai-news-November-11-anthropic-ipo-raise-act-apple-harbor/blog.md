## Anthropic's S-1 Puts a $1.4 Trillion Price on Safety

### A $1.4 Trillion Target

Anthropic updated its S-1 on November 10, 2026, with an initial price range of $70 to $78 per share. The company is targeting a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion. It first filed [to go public in late October](/blog/ai-news-October-27-openai-eth-anthropic-s1-terra).

### Seven Founders, One Share Count

Each of the seven co-founders plans to sell 3,378,378 shares. At the $74 midpoint, that is just under $250 million per person. The range runs from about $236 million at $70 to about $264 million at $78.

### The Founder Payout at Three Prices

| IPO price | Per founder | All seven |
|---|---|---|
| $70 | $236.5M | $1.66B |
| $74 | $250.0M | $1.75B |
| $78 | $263.5M | $1.84B |

The table assumes all 3,378,378 shares sell at the stated price. It excludes taxes and underwriting costs.

### Reed Hastings Wants $300 Million

Board member Reed Hastings has indicated he wants to buy $300 million of shares through a donor-advised fund. Board member Christopher Liddell wants $3 million. That demand sits alongside the founder sales.

### Control Stays With the Founders

After the sale, each founder will own about 3.2% of Class A shares, or roughly 22% together. Class A is only one share class. Amazon and Google hold nonvoting Class N shares, so the founders' total equity stake is smaller. Each founder also gets one Class F share with supervoting rights. Together they will control 51.1% of votes.

### The Safety Premium Meets the Market

Anthropic has sold itself as the safety-first lab since 2021. The IPO tests whether public investors pay a premium for that story. [Its junk credit rating](/blog/ai-news-October-31-anthropic-junk-black-october-orbit) shows the bond market is less romantic.

## New York Opens the RAISE Act Registration Portal

### The Clock Starts Now

New York opened its RAISE Act registration portal on November 10, 2026. Large frontier developers must register by January 1, 2027. The law covers companies with more than $500 million in annual revenue.

### Three Public Obligations

Registered labs must publish safety frameworks on their websites. They must report critical safety incidents within 72 hours. They must also issue public model transparency reports.

### Fines of $1 Million, Then $3 Million

The new Office of Digital Innovation, Governance, Integrity, and Trust will enforce the law. Penalties can reach $1 million for a first violation and $3 million for later ones. The state can also act against companies that skip registration.

### Hochul's Warning to the Industry

Governor Kathy Hochul framed the law as a brake on the race for capability. "They tell us get on board or get left behind," she said. "That any collateral damage is worth it as long as we win the race to the AI cliff."

### A Research Center at Binghamton

Hochul also launched the New York Center for AI Responsibility and Research at SUNY Binghamton. The center will evaluate advanced AI systems and help review safety frameworks. The state first announced it in January with $55 million in donations and capital.

### More Laws Are Coming

New York already enforces a companion-bot disclosure law. A separate chatbot safety law for minors takes effect on June 1, 2027. Other states have passed similar safety and transparency bills, so national labs now face a patchwork.

## Nvidia's Board Hires Wachtell as the Stock Slips

### A 9.5% Two-Day Drop

Nvidia's stock fell 5.47% on Tuesday after a 4% drop on Monday. The two-day loss is about 9.5%, and no product announcement explains it. The selloff follows disclosures about CEO Jensen Huang's affair with Alice Huang.

### The Spy Question

U.S. intelligence agencies believe Alice Huang was spying for China, according to TechCrunch. Huang has admitted the extramarital affair. Nvidia's board says it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised.

### Wachtell Gets the File

The board hired Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to run an internal investigation. Independent directors are overseeing the work with their own outside counsel. Corporate governance professors say there is no clear precedent for a CEO caught in a suspected honeypot.

### Three Questions for the Board

Ann Lipton of the University of Colorado lists three lines of inquiry. Did Huang break the law, did he violate company policy, and was the board misled? Todd Henderson of the University of Chicago says the key fact is what Alice Huang actually obtained.

### The Timing Problem

Wired reported that Huang hired a PR crisis firm known for sex scandals a year ago. It is unclear when he learned Alice Huang was a suspected spy. That timeline will shape whether the board can keep him.

### What Comes Next

Nvidia is due to brief Congress on November 18. The board's findings and the congressional record will decide whether this stays a reputational crisis or becomes a legal one. ## Apple's Harbor Turns a Mac into a Private Inference Server

### A Claris App in Private Preview

Leaker Aaron Perris found evidence of a new Mac app called Harbor. It sits under Apple's Claris brand and is in private preview. Apple has not announced it publicly.

### Your Data Stays on the Desk

Harbor runs open-weight models on Apple silicon. The pitch is simple: prompts, documents, and outputs never leave the hardware. Nothing goes to a cloud provider.

### Thunderbolt 5 Pools Memory

Harbor can connect several Macs over Thunderbolt 5. The linked machines pool unified memory, which allows larger models and more concurrent users. A single Mac has a fixed memory ceiling; pooling raises it.

### The Local Server Thesis

Apple has spent years putting Neural Engines in every Mac. Harbor would turn that installed base into a fleet of inference boxes. It also fits Apple's privacy marketing better than a cloud API.

### What Is Still Missing

There is no price, launch date, or supported model list. The leak shows a private preview, not a product. Treat Harbor as a signal that Apple wants a piece of local inference.

## Anthropic Turns Claude into a Meeting Participant

### A Bot With a Seat at the Table

Anthropic launched Claude Meetings in beta on November 10, 2026. Teams can add Claude to a Google Meet call as a visible participant. It follows the conversation and drafts the plan while people talk.

### Work Happens Before the Call Ends

Claude can map a timeline on a whiteboard, answer questions in chat, and pull figures from connected apps. After the call, notes and action items land in claude.ai.

### The Guest Rules

Claude identifies itself and posts a notice in the meeting chat. It will not join calls with external guests unless an admin authorizes it. If an external guest joins, Claude leaves automatically.

### Whiteboards and Sheets

Claude Whiteboards is a shared canvas that turns a brainstorm into a timeline. Claude can export the board as a doc or slide deck. Claude Sheets builds working spreadsheets with tabs, formulas, and dropdowns. Both are in beta on paid plans.

### The Rest of the Office Suite

Claude Data, formerly Claude Dashboards, is now generally available. It returns dashboards, written data stories, and narrated videos. Interactive connectors also open Adobe, Atlassian, and HubSpot apps inside Claude.

### Perplexity's $200 Teammates

Perplexity launched Teammates, always-on agents that take multiple roles in a workspace. They pull projects, skills, and memory from existing Perplexity tools. The feature requires the $200-per-month Max plan. OpenAI's Dots and Claude Duet are chasing the same always-on coworker idea.

## Gemini 4 Carbon Goes Public With an Agent Swarm

### From Defenders to Everyone

Google released Gemini 4 Carbon to the public on November 10, 2026. It spent the previous two weeks with a select group of cybersecurity defenders. Google calls Carbon its strongest model yet.

### Built for the Hardest Tasks

Google says Carbon goes beyond Argon, which launched in October. Argon will likely remain the better choice for most software engineering and knowledge work. Carbon targets complex multi-step coding and open-ended scientific research.

### The Introductory Price

Carbon costs $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens at launch. Those prices double at the end of November. Google says that is about a fifth of GPT-6 Astra and a quarter of Claude Fable 5.2.

### An Agent Swarm in Antigravity

Google is previewing a swarm feature on its Antigravity platform. A single prompt can become a coordinated team of agents. That is the same multi-agent pattern Anthropic and OpenAI are pushing.

### Where You Can Use It

Paying developers get Carbon through the Gemini API, AI Studio, and Gemini for business. Google AI Ultra subscribers get it in the Gemini app, AI Mode in Search, and Antigravity. Free and lower-tier users have no announced date.

### The Benchmark Caveat

Google's own benchmarks place Carbon behind Claude Fable 5.2 on most metrics. [Fable 5.2's launch](/blog/ai-news-November-10-fable-5-2-tpu-8tx-notion-breach) set a new bar on the Artificial Analysis index. Carbon competes on price, not on every score.

**Sources:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1030787/google-gemini-4-carbon-public-rollout), [Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-carbon-now-available/)

## Credit Markets Send a Warning to AI Builders

### A 16% Yield on CoreWeave Debt

CoreWeave's 2032 bond carries a 9.625% coupon. Its yield has climbed above 16%, according to CNBC. That means buyers demand a huge discount to hold the debt.

### Insurance Costs Are Rising Everywhere

CoreWeave's five-year credit default swaps spiked above 1,289 basis points last week. Nvidia's CDS is up 76% in a month, Broadcom's 49%, and Amazon's 44%. The absolute spreads remain low for the giants, but the direction is clear.

### The Credit Card Analogy

Jefferies strategist Christopher Wood put the risk plainly. "The most plausible way the AI capex arms race will end is that the credit card will be taken away." Debt markets, not model benchmarks, may set the pace of 2027.

### Politics Meets Data Centers

New Democratic governors in Ohio, Texas, and Georgia may slow permits. Goldman Sachs says moratoriums could touch 45% of planned US capacity. Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa wants a pause until the legislature passes community protections.

### The Debt Bill Keeps Growing

Hyperscalers have issued $296 billion of debt this year, per Goldman. The bank forecasts $440 billion next year, plus $300 billion of project debt. Higher yields make every gigawatt more expensive.

## Australia Weighs Criminal Charges for AI Companies

### A Response to the Medicare Hack

Australia is considering a criminal offence for AI companies that fail to prevent harmful conduct. The proposal responds to September's OpenAI hack of the Medicare statistics portal. The government accepted all recommendations from its review.

### A World-First Failure to Prevent Offence

No other country has a national criminal offence built around an AI company's duty to prevent harm. The offence would apply when a model causes harm and the company did not take reasonable steps to stop it. Australia is also considering penalties for egregious breaches of safety standards.

### A Watchdog With Real Fines

The plan includes an AI safety watchdog empowered to issue significant fines. The government expects to release the rest of its AI standards by the end of the year. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said the law must catch up with a fast-changing world.

### The Proof Problem

Monash professor Michael Duffy said proving foreseeability and fault will be difficult. UTS professor Ed Santow argued civil penalties may be more useful than criminal ones. He compared the scale to workplace safety law.

### OpenAI Is Reviewing the Proposals

OpenAI said it is reviewing the government's proposals. Anthropic, Google, and Meta did not respond to requests for comment. The AFP has received no formal report of a crime and has opened no criminal investigation.
