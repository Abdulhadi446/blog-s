## PSI Proves the Aaronson-Ambainis Conjecture With an Anthropic Model

### A 2008 Question About Quantum Speedups

Scott Aaronson and Andris Ambainis posed the question in 2008. It asks when quantum computers can be exponentially faster on random inputs. Quantum speedups like Shor's factoring rely on hidden structure. Random strings almost never have such structure.

### Aaronson and Ambainis Reduced It to Pure Math

They stated a conjecture about polynomials and boolean functions. If true, an ordinary computer can imitate a quantum one. The number of input "looks" grows only polynomially in T. Exponential quantum speedups would then require rare input patterns.

### PSI's System Found the Proof in Three Days

PSI developed the research system internally. It ran on PSI's own infrastructure. Non-public Anthropic model instances powered the run. The system held a complete proof by the third day. Lean, a proof assistant, checked every step.

### The Bound and the Constant

PSI proved that influence is at least `c · v^(5/2) / d^6`, and at least `(v/d)^7`. The constant `c` equals `815 / (125π^4)`, about 0.0025. Earlier bounds for general polynomials decayed exponentially in the degree.

### Independent Readers and Auditors

Guy Blanc, Jordan Docter, Carmen Strassle, and Li-Yang Tan settled a special case in August 2026. They are now reading PSI's full proof. They called the result a huge one. David Turturean led the work and is responsible for correctness. Jim Halverson audited the proof. Phiala Shanahan commented on the Lean formalization.

### What It Means for Quantum Computing

Quantum computers cannot save exponentially many looks on yes-or-no questions. That holds unless the input has a rare pattern. One long-open question is now settled. The proof paper and Lean code are public.

## OpenSSH Bug Opens Password-Disabled Linux Accounts

### The '*sshole' Flaw on musl Systems

Attackers can abuse a flaw dubbed "*sshole". It affects OpenSSH on musl-based Linux systems. A password of 256 or more characters can log in. It works against accounts that had passwords disabled. Alpine and similar musl distributions face direct risk. Administrators should patch immediately.

### What Admins Should Do Now

Update OpenSSH packages across all systems. Audit for password-disabled accounts. Review authentication configuration. Check vendor advisories for affected versions. Rotate keys if compromise is suspected. Test fallback login paths. The flaw highlights review debt in auth code.

**Sources:** [AI Weekly alert — OpenSSH flaw](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/openssh-sshole-flaw-opens-musl-linux-accounts-to-attackers)
